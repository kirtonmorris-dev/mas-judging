You already know where this goes wrong.

Every committee runs into the same three moments, whether it's a steelband semifinal or a school talent show.

Paper sheets get lost or smudged.

Scores travel by hand between judges and a tabulation table, and there's no record of what happened in between.

Totals get added by hand, under pressure.

A rushed manual tally, with a crowd waiting, is exactly where a legitimate placement gets challenged later.

Results wait on whoever has the calculator.

The contestant who just performed, and everyone in the room, ends up waiting far longer than the performance itself.

How It Works

Three steps, from PIN entry to a printed result.

1

Judges score on their phone.

They log in with a PIN, no app to install, and score each contestant against your own criteria and point scale.

2

Results tally live.

Organizers watch the tally update the moment each judge submits, with any drop-high, drop-low, or averaging rule applied automatically.

3

Print the official sign-off sheet.

Close out the category and print a signed record for the podium, with no transcription step in between.

Why It's Different

Built around your rules, not a generic form.

Every competition judges differently. Judge D Show scores to your categories, your criteria and your point splits, with a live tally while it happens and a printed sign-off sheet at the end.

No score is ever lost.

Load-tested at 20 simultaneous judges scoring at once, with zero errors and no overwritten submissions.

You know the moment the signal drops.

The app detects when a judge goes offline and warns them immediately, and any save that fails gets a one-tap retry the moment they're back online — no guessing whether a score actually went through.

One founder, in the room.

Kirt is present in person on the night of your event, not just reachable by phone or WhatsApp from somewhere else.

Your criteria, your points.

Blind by design.

Close judging, lock the scores.

When scoring ends, the organizer closes judging and scores lock. Any later correction needs a written reason on the record.
